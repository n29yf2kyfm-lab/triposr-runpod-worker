## 1. Bricks — bonds, types, money

Sources: designingbuildings.co.uk types-of-brick-bonding;
traditionalbrickandstone.co.uk; imperialbricks.co.uk cost guide;
tradewarehouse.co.uk; checkatrade cost-of-bricks-per-1000.

Bonds (pattern = cost as well as look):
- **Stretcher bond** — the modern cavity-wall default; fewest cuts,
  fastest to lay, least waste. What every volume housebuilder uses.
- **Flemish bond** — header+stretcher alternating in each course; the
  Georgian signature. On a modern cavity wall it is done with snapped
  headers — more labour, more waste, noticeably dearer. Specify for
  heritage-matching front elevations only.
- **English bond** — alternating header/stretcher COURSES; strongest,
  engineering/civils look.
- **English garden wall** — 3 stretcher courses per header course; the
  budget way to get "traditional" texture.
- **Stack bond** — non-structural, needs reinforcement, extra cutting.

Brick types, per 1000 (supply only, 2025-26):
- Common bricks £250–400 — unseen work.
- Facing bricks £400–800 — the elevation standard; wire-cut cheapest,
  stock bricks mid, waterstruck top.
- Engineering (Class A blue / Class B red) £500–900 — below dpc,
  plinths, copings; the West Midlands blue-brick plinth look.
- Handmade/reclaimed £800–3900 — heritage matching.
- Rule of thumb: ~60 bricks/m² in stretcher bond (half-brick leaf);
  a 3-bed detached front elevation ≈ 3,500–5,000 bricks.

## 2. Timber

Sources: buildwiz.uk C16 guide; ryedaletimber.co.uk C16-vs-C24;
buildersmerchantsdirect.co.uk timber prices; kelvintimber/southern-timber
CLS listings; forestresearch.gov.uk price indices.

- **C16** — the domestic default (studwork, noggins, standard joists).
- **C24** — 10–25% dearer, stiffer, for longer spans/heavier loads;
  specify only where the span tables demand it.
- **CLS** (Canadian Lumber Standard, planed, eased edges): 38×89 for
  internal studs, 38×140 for timber-frame external studs; from ~£1.66/m.
- Pressure treatment adds 15–25%; required for sole plates, anything
  external or in contact with masonry.
- Softwood structural timber ≈ £400–600/m³ (2025-26).
- Sheet: OSB3 is the structural budget default (sarking, deck, sheathing);
  plywood stiffer but dearer; 18 mm OSB3 2440×1220 is the benchmark sheet.
- Engineered (I-joists, LVL, posi-joists): dearer per metre but straight,
  light, span further, pre-drilled for services — often cheaper INSTALLED.

## 3. Plastics, glazing, claddings, renders (the skin options)

Sources: windowpriceonline.co.uk; checkatrade window & cladding guides;
myjobquote.co.uk cladding costs; homebuilding.co.uk cladding cost;
webster-srs.com render rates.

- **uPVC windows** (the volume standard): casement £300–550 fitted;
  sash-look £520–800; bay £1,000–2,400. Aluminium roughly 1.5–2×,
  timber 2–3×. White cheapest; foiled colours (anthracite) +10–20%.
- **Cladding, fitted £/m²**: uPVC ~£80–150 (cheapest installed);
  fibre cement (Cedral) ~£80–200; timber £90–180 (larch/thermowood mid,
  featheredge board cheapest material at ~£20/m² supply); composite
  £120–220.
- **Render on blockwork**: sand-cement ~£10/m² material, monocouche
  £30–60/m², silicone (K-Rend/Parex) £40–80/m² — self-coloured, no
  painting cycle. Render is the cheapest way to change a facade's look.
- Whole 3-bed semi reskin (90–120 m² walls): ~£9k–22k depending system.

## 4. Merchants — where the money actually goes

Sources: buildersmerchantsdirect.co.uk; merchant listings surveyed
(Travis Perkins, Jewson, Wickes, B&Q, Selco, Huws Gray, MGN, LBS).

- Chains (Travis Perkins, Jewson, Selco, Buildbase): trade accounts,
  credit, delivery, haggling expected — list price is an opening offer.
- Sheds (B&Q, Wickes): retail pricing, fine for smalls/one-offs,
  generally beaten by merchants on volume.
- **Independents undercut national chains 10–20% on timber and heavy
  side** — and negotiate on 30+ lengths. Price the PACKAGE (bricks +
  blocks + aggregates + cement) with 2–3 merchants per job.
- Reclaimed yards for heritage brick/slate matching.

## 5. Build method and whole-build cost (2025-26 benchmarks)

Sources: self-build.co.uk build costs; ukcalculator.com; homehow.co.uk;
bestbuilders.co.uk timber-frame comparison; RLB timber-vs-masonry study;
selfbuildextendrenovate.co.uk.

- New build: £1,400–3,000/m² (standard spec £2,000–2,500/m²;
  West Midlands near national average; London +25–35%).
- Extensions: budget single-storey from ~£1,500/m², standard
  £2,000–2,800/m²; **two-storey is CHEAPER per m² (£1,600–2,400)** —
  one foundation and one roof serve two floors.
- Masonry (brick+block) vs timber frame: near cost parity per RLB-type
  studies (~£1,056 vs £1,067/m² structure-only in one dataset); timber
  frame 20–30% faster on site (finance saving), masonry more familiar
  to small builders and easier for staged self-build.
- **The cheap-build playbook** (what actually cuts cost):
  1. Simple rectangular plan, two full storeys, gable roof — minimum
     wall and roof per m² of floor (our 3-bed demo shape is right).
  2. Standard spans ≤ 4.2 m so C16 joists and off-the-shelf trusses.
  3. Stretcher-bond facing brick or render-on-block; save feature brick
     for the front elevation only.
  4. uPVC casements in standard sizes (made-to-measure premium avoided).
  5. Wet rooms stacked on one service riser (our plan does this).
  6. Fenestration on the alignment grid — repeats mean bulk prices.
  7. Trussed rafters not cut roofs; eaves kept simple.
  8. Groundworks are the risk pot: flat site + strip founds = cheap;
     trees/clay/slopes eat budgets (see GROUND_AND_FLOW.md).

## 6. Social housing — the standards that bite

Sources: NDSS gov.uk PDF; london.gov.uk Housing Design Standards LPG
2023; tandfonline space-standards-in-affordable-housing paper; Parker
Morris history (wikipedia).

- History: Parker Morris (1961) mandatory for councils 1969–1980,
  abolished 1980; NDSS (2015) is its successor — optional nationally,
  ADOPTED via local plans and REQUIRED by most funders.
- Homes England / GLA funding in practice requires NDSS compliance;
  London Plan makes it policy with 10 m² ceiling-height and private
  outdoor space additions (5 m² per 1–2 person dwelling).
- Practical consequence: a social-housing plan is checked against NDSS
  GIA + bedroom sizes + storage + 2.3–2.5 m ceiling heights as HARD
  gates, plus M4(2) accessible dwellings (step-free approach, wider
  doors 850 clear, future stair-lift provision) at a set percentage.
- Our pipeline: NDSS bedroom/GIA checks fit `regs.py` as a rule pack;
  M4(2) door-width check already half-exists (PART_M_CLEAR_M 0.775 —
  note M4(2) asks more than M4(1)).
